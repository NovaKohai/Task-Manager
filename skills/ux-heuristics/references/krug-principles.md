# Krug's Usability Principles

Full methodology from "Don't Make Me Think" for creating intuitive, usable interfaces.

## The Reality of How Users Use the Web

### Fact 1: We Don't Read, We Scan
- Users scan, not read
- Create visual hierarchy, use headings, keep paragraphs short, use bulleted lists

### Fact 2: We Don't Make Optimal Choices, We Satisfice
- Users click the first reasonable option, not the best option
- Make the right choice obvious

### Fact 3: We Don't Figure Out How Things Work, We Muddle Through
- Users try things until something works
- Make things obvious, design for recovery

## Things You Should Never Do
- Don't Make Users Think (puzzling labels, unexplained options, jargon)
- Don't Waste Users' Goodwill (hiding info, punishing wrong ways, asking unnecessary info)
- Don't Make Words Seem Important If They're Not (remove "happy talk")

## Navigation Must-Haves
- Site ID, Page name, Sections, Local navigation, Utilities, "You are here" indicator
- The Trunk Test: can users answer what site, what page, what sections, where am I, how to search?
- Breadcrumbs: Home > Products > Shoes

## Homepage Guidelines
- 3-4 seconds to answer: What is this? What can I do here? Why should I be here?
- Tagline: conveys unique value, specific, 6-8 words

## Mobile Usability
- 44x44px minimum tap targets, 16px minimum body text
- Hamburger or bottom nav, break long forms into steps

## Usability Testing on $0
- 3-4 users catches most issues
- Test protocol: "I'm testing the site, not you", "Think out loud", don't help

## Accessibility Basics
- 15-20% of population has some disability
- Quick wins: alt text, contrast, keyboard nav, semantic HTML, focus indicators
